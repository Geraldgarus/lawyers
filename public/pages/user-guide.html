The Core Idea: Everything Lives Inside a Case

A Case is the hub for one legal matter — its Parties, the Versus (opponent), and every Court Date, document, expense, invoice, and note tied to it. All of that is created from inside the case, on Time Chart and Court Records → open a case. The Calendar, Documents, Expenses, Billing & Payments, and Reports menus are just views that pull everything together across every case so you can search and browse — they are not separate places you enter new data (Expenses is the one exception; see below).

Opening a New Case

  1. Go to Time Chart and Court Records in the sidebar and click + New Case.
  2. Give it a Case Number, then fill in the full Time Chart form: the court and region, the presiding judge, the parties and their counsel, the court's last order and current prayer, and the next court date. This first entry doubles as the case's opening Court Date record.
  3. Click Create Case — you're taken straight to the case page, where everything else happens.
There's no separate client database to register someone in first — the parties' names are typed straight into the case.

Recording a Court Date

  1. Open the case and go to its Court Dates tab, then click Add Court Date.
  2. Fill in that visit's Time Chart form the same way as a new case — presiding judge, court order, next court date, and so on.
  3. The most recent one shows on the case's Overview tab as "Last Court Appearance," and its Next Court Date automatically appears on the Calendar and the Dashboard as it approaches.
  4. Use View or Print on any entry to see it laid out as the formal Time Chart and Court Records document.

Calendar

Shows every case's Court Dates and any Appointments you've logged, grouped by day. Court Dates come from the cases themselves; to add an appointment (e.g. a client consultation), click Add Appointment — you can optionally link it to an existing case, or leave it as a general consultation.

Documents

To upload a document, open the relevant case → Documents tab → Upload, and pick a category (Contract, Court Order, Evidence, Judgment, Correspondence, or Other). The Documents page in the sidebar is a read-only view across every case — filter it by case or category, then download from there.

Expenses

Unlike documents and invoices, expenses can be logged two ways: from inside a case's Expenses tab (tied to that case), or directly from the Expenses page in the sidebar using Add Expense and leaving the case field as "No case" for a general office expense. Either way it shows up in both places and in Reports.

Billing a Client

  1. Open the case, go to its Payments tab, and click New Invoice.
  2. When the client pays (in full or partially), click the money icon next to the invoice to record the payment.
  3. The outstanding balance updates automatically and shows on the case Overview.
The Billing & Payments page in the sidebar is a read-only view across every case's invoices — new invoices are only created from inside a case.

Notes & the Timeline Tab

Use a case's Notes tab for private thoughts or meeting summaries that don't belong anywhere else. Its Timeline tab is generated automatically — every Court Date, document, expense, payment, and note you add to the case shows up there in order, with no extra effort.

Reports & Case Status Report

Reports gives a financial overview (income, expenses, net profit) and a breakdown of cases by status, over a date range you choose. Case Status Report is a separate, manually maintained record — it does not pull from the cases above. Use Add Entry to type in a case's number, parties, court, a narrative summary, claim amount, status notes, and remarks, matching the firm's paper "Status of Cases Handled" form; each entry has its own Print button, and a case number can only be recorded once.

Settings

Office information (name, contact details, currency symbol), the list of Case Statuses a case can be set to (and whether each one counts as closed), Expense Categories, and account lockout security thresholds are all managed here.

User Management, Roles & Permissions

  • User Management — create staff accounts, assign each one a role, reset passwords, unlock, deactivate, or delete an account. Roles themselves aren't fixed — Add Role here lets you create a new one (e.g. "Paralegal"); a brand-new role starts with no capabilities until you grant it some.
  • Permissions & Access — the Role Capability Matrix is what actually controls what each role can do (create/delete cases, manage billing, view reports, and so on) — check a box and save that role's row to grant it. User Page Access further restricts one specific individual to only certain pages, on top of their role.

The admin account always has full access to everything and isn't shown in the matrix.
